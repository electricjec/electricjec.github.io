ANTIPARALLEL

A custom analog overdrive pedal that keeps the guitar signal analog while adding digital awareness, visual feedback, and a completely custom hardware platform.

Analog Electronics PCB Design Embedded Systems Audio Firmware

OVERVIEW

Rebuilding an analog classic as something of my own.

ANTIPARALLEL is a custom guitar overdrive pedal I designed around an analog op-amp overdrive architecture with multiple selectable clipping characteristics.

Instead of treating it as just another distortion circuit, I used the project as a platform to combine analog electronics, embedded systems, PCB design, user-interface development, enclosure design, and physical product development.

The audio path stays analog. The digital electronics exist alongside it to monitor controls, drive the display, and make the pedal more informative without replacing the analog signal processing that defines its sound.

THE IDEA

Analog where sound matters.
Digital where information matters.

The concept behind ANTIPARALLEL was to preserve the character and immediacy of a traditional analog overdrive while introducing modern interaction around it.

ANALOG SIGNAL PATH

The sound never has to become digital.

The guitar signal is processed through an analog overdrive circuit built around an op-amp gain stage, diode clipping, tone shaping, and output-level control.

This separation is intentional. The embedded system can observe control positions and provide visual feedback, but it does not replace the core analog signal path.

CLIPPING SYSTEM

Three ways to shape the same analog signal.

Different diode configurations allow the clipping behavior of the overdrive stage to be changed without rebuilding the core circuit.

01

Smooth

A softer clipping character designed for a smoother and more compressed response.

02

Standard

The central overdrive voice and the reference point for the pedal's classic clipping behavior.

03

Turbo

A more aggressive mode intended to produce a stronger and more pronounced clipping response.

EMBEDDED MONITORING

The knobs remain physical.
The pedal knows where they are.

A microcontroller subsystem monitors the pedal's controls and translates their positions into information that can be shown on the display.

Physical control first.

The pedal still behaves like a physical effects pedal. Gain, tone, volume, and mode selection remain direct controls that can be adjusted immediately.

Information second.

The screen adds awareness of parameter positions and operating state without turning the pedal into a menu-driven digital interface.

INTERFACE

Designed to still feel like a guitar pedal.

Gain

Controls the amount of overdrive produced by the analog gain stage.

Tone

Shapes the tonal response of the overdriven signal from darker to brighter settings.

Volume

Sets the final output level after clipping and tone shaping.

Mode

Selects between the available clipping configurations and their corresponding indicator states.

PCB DEVELOPMENT

From spaghetti wiring
to a purpose-built PCB.

Once the circuit architecture had been proven through prototyping, the electronics were translated into a dedicated PCB for a cleaner and more repeatable hardware build.

Layout

Components were arranged around the physical constraints of the enclosure, control locations, connectors, display, and analog signal path.

Fabrication

The design was prepared as manufacturing-ready board files, professionally fabricated, assembled, and tested as part of the final pedal.

DEVELOPMENT

The enclosure was only the last layer.

01

Circuit prototype

The analog overdrive circuit was first developed and tested before being integrated with the rest of the system.

02

Embedded subsystem

Control sensing, display behavior, and the supporting microcontroller electronics were developed alongside the analog section.

03

PCB integration

The working prototype was converted into a dedicated board designed around the actual mechanical layout.

04

Enclosure and assembly

Electronics, controls, display, switches, indicators, and external connections were assembled into the finished pedal enclosure.

05

Calibration and refinement

Control sensing, display mapping, operating behavior, and physical interaction were refined after the complete system was assembled.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGES

Making every subsystem behave as one product.

01

Analog + digital integration

Embedded electronics had to coexist with a sensitive analog audio path without compromising the pedal's primary function.

02

Control calibration

Physical control positions did not always map linearly to displayed values, requiring calibration and software-side compensation.

03

Mechanical integration

The PCB, display, switches, knobs, connectors, wiring, and enclosure all had to align physically while remaining practical to assemble and service.

WHAT I LEARNED

A circuit can work.
A product has to work together.

ANTIPARALLEL taught me that building hardware goes far beyond getting an individual circuit to function. Analog electronics, firmware, PCB layout, mechanical constraints, calibration, user interaction, and assembly decisions all affect the final result.

It became one of the projects that pushed me from simply prototyping circuits toward thinking about complete physical products.
